This famous tower is still standing today because its builders stopped building it.
It's 1173, and the rich port city of Pisa is building the whitest, tallest bell tower anyone has ever seen.
The workers stack up one marble floor, then another, then a third.
Every floor adds more weight, and the ground underneath starts to give.
Then somebody notices the tower is already leaning.
So the builders do the only sensible thing, and just stop.
Nobody is totally sure why, but Pisa was busy fighting wars with its neighbours.
Luckily, the city was too busy fighting wars to keep building.
Unluckily, the city was too busy fighting wars.
You see, soft clay works like a wet sponge.
Ever stood on a wet beach? Your feet sink in, the water squeezes out, and the sand underneath packs down firmer.
The tower had been squeezing the water out of the clay, and the clay needed time to get strong again.
A hundred years later, the builders came back to ground that had finally toughened up.
They made one side of every new floor a little taller, to fight the lean, which gave the tower a slight banana curve.
Today it still leans, and in 2008 engineers announced it had finally stopped moving.
Their secret was digging soil out from under the high side.
So the tower was saved by quitting, and then saved again by digging a hole.
