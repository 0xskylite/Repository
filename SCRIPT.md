## Facts and sources
- Work on the bell tower began in 1173; the foundation was only about 3 m deep, set in soft clay. (Wikipedia, Leaning Tower of Pisa; Ingenia, Prof. J. Burland)
- The tilt began once the third floor was built (c. 1178) and work stopped. (ScienceABC; Atlas Piers)
- Why work stopped is not known for certain; the usual explanation is Pisa's near-constant wars with Genoa, Lucca and Florence. (Ingenia; Wikipedia)
- Work resumed c. 1272, nearly 100 years later, when the clay had gained strength by consolidating under the tower's weight; had building continued, the tower would probably have fallen over. (Ingenia, Burland)
- Builders made one side of each upper floor taller to correct the lean, giving the tower a slight curve. (Wikipedia; Atlas Piers)
- Tilt reached 5.5 degrees by 1990. In May 2008 engineers announced it had stopped moving for the first time in its history, after soil was removed from under the raised side. (Wikipedia)
- Direction of lean is left out on purpose (sources disagree on wording). Mussolini 1934 concrete story left out (weak source).
